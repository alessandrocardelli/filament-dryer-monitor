# One priority — true, independently verified characterization of the embedded heater NTC

**User requirement, 22 November 2026:** obtain a precise, repeatable and defensible relationship between the actual NTC's electrical resistance and *its own* temperature, before relying on that relationship to control or protect the heater. The fixed Sanwa plate-contact probe must **not** be removed or repositioned. The Sanwa is not proven defective; its former contact-equivalent curve cannot be used as intrinsic thermistor calibration.

## Stage A — verify that NTC ohms reported by ESP32 are actual ohms

**Purpose:** independently establish measurement-chain error around the historical 7,120Ω trip. This tests ESP32 ADC, resistor divider, GPIO34, harness/connector readout, not the thermistor's R(T). It is not a fan-only or another long drying trial.

**Proposed one-time procedure, after firmware/instrument review and explicit operator agreement:** leave Sanwa physically untouched. With power isolated and heating not enabled, disconnect only the two-wire thermistor plug J7; replace its input temporarily with individually DMM-measured, appropriate resistors near **6.8, 8.2, 10 and 12 kΩ**. Using the project's existing ADC circuit and read-only firmware diagnostic, record for each sample: actual DMM Ω, uncorrected ADC mV, corrected mV, firmware Ω, absolute and percent error, measurement spread, supply voltage, test date/instrument. Reconnect original J7 afterward. The source supports passive readout, but the specific measurement workflow must be validated before instructing an upload or wiring operation. **No modification of Sanwa and no heating during electrical substitution.**

**Completion criterion:** characterize measured error and variation at each four known resistances, then either correct ADC conversion with independently confirmed parameters and recheck on an unfit intermediate resistance, or confirm it meets a specified error budget. Do not assume an arbitrary ±1% error before seeing actual results. Run no HEATER trial until an adequate electrical readout is established.

## Stage B — obtain the actual NTC resistance ↔ temperature relationship

**Requirement:** a temperature reference of known/calibrated accuracy must be in thermal equilibrium with the **NTC bead/body**, not merely a different surface point during a transient heat-up. A controlled homogeneous/isothermal thermal environment for the heater+NTC assembly (if mechanically/electrically safe and feasible) and a calibrated probe, e.g. a calibrated PT100/PT1000 with a suitable reader, are required for a meaningful absolute-temperature calibration. A controlled bath is acceptable **only for a bare, compatible isolated sensor**, never for an assembled powered heater, PCB, TCO or plastic housing. A normal domestic oven with the complete dryer is not a safe or controlled substitute. Do not remove the fixed Sanwa just to satisfy this plan.

**Exact proposed data collection once an appropriate fixture/reference is established:** at least six temperature equilibrium points covering the intended local heater-temperature range, provisionally ~20, 35, 50, 65, 80, 90°C (do not extend higher without validated fixture/material/TCO envelope), in increasing and decreasing order; hold each until reference and NTC resistance readings stabilize (stability/time criterion must be defined from instrument capabilities); log calibrated reference °C, direct NTC resistance Ω with low measurement current, elapsed time, thermal gradients/probe positions and uncertainty. Repeat on a second independent heating/cooling pass if safe to establish repeatability; leave at least one point out of the fitted model for independent prediction-error validation. Report raw points, residuals, worst-case observed error in °C, repeatability and range of validity; fit an appropriate R(T) model such as Steinhart–Hart, not a guessed R25/beta.

**Feasibility gate:** because the original NTC is embedded in an OEM heater module, its body is not independently accessible to the currently mounted Sanwa. Before promising precision or proposing another physical heating operation, establish whether the **heater/NTC module** can be subjected to a genuinely uniform, controlled temperature with an independent reference. If not, an in-situ plate-contact correlation (even using a better external probe) cannot provide a known-accuracy intrinsic NTC calibration. Remaining engineering options are to obtain the OEM thermistor's actual identified manufacturer R(T) datasheet with an independent check, have the isolated sensor/module characterized by suitable lab equipment, or replace the sensor with a specified temperature element in an independently qualified installation. These are alternatives, NOT silently approved hardware changes.

**Safety and scope:** no series TCO changes; no loosening of hard or soft NTC fault boundaries; no new heater-enabled sketch or repeated AIR55 run authorized by this plan. Pre-existing 7,120Ω trip, 7,120Ω old soft pause, 8,236.6Ω old resume and later 7,923.8/9,231.9Ω contact-proxy controls are **PROVISIONAL_EXPERIMENT**, not independently calibrated temperature bounds. 105°C reported installed TCO is hardware, not a qualified fixture limit. No physical test should be scheduled until actual feasibility, adequate reference instruments, safety and explicit operator approval are confirmed.

**Acceptance:** independent electrical-chain accuracy in the critical 7–12kΩ region plus defensible true R(T) points and uncertainty/repeatability, or explicit engineering statement of why this cannot be established on the existing assembled sensor. Only then select thermostat/fault-temperature thresholds and evaluate a targeted attended warm verification. 

**Older fan-only code:** [optional diagnostic](../firmware/diagnostics/ntc_fan_only/ntc_fan_only.ino) is NOT the current priority and is untested. The previous plan to immediately flash the 90/85 control and repeat AIR55 without true R(T) was superseded; nothing was flashed or re-energized.
